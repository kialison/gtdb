Event Contracts allow users to trade on the outcomes of real-world events. Users do not bet against the platform; instead, they buy and sell outcome shares with other users in an open peer-to-peer market. Prices reflect the market participants' collective estimate of the probability that an outcome will occur.

## Price as Probability

Each outcome share in an Event Contract is priced between `0.00` and `1.00`. The price can be interpreted as the market's estimated probability of that outcome occurring.

For example, if a market's "Yes" share trades at `$0.65`, the market is currently estimating roughly a 65% probability that the event will occur.

## Collateral and Outcome Tokens

Event Contracts use USDT as collateral. Each Yes / No outcome pair is fully collateralized:

- `1 USDT` can correspond to 1 Yes share and 1 No share;
- the winning outcome share can be redeemed for `$1.00`;
- the losing outcome share becomes worthless.

Outcome shares are represented using the [Gnosis Conditional Token Framework](https://docs.gnosis.io/conditionaltokens/) (ERC-1155), enabling on-chain trading and settlement.

## Trading

Event Contracts use a peer-to-peer Central Limit Order Book (CLOB). Users trade directly with other users rather than with the platform as a counterparty.

- If you believe the market is underestimating an outcome's probability, you can buy shares of that outcome.
- If you believe the market is overestimating an outcome's probability, you can sell shares of that outcome.
- Before final settlement, you can sell a position at any time to lock in profit or limit losses.

| Action | When to Use It | Profitable When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy Yes | You believe the current probability is too low | The event occurs |
| Buy No | You believe the current probability is too high | The event does not occur |
| Sell | Lock in profit or limit losses | Price moves in your favor |

## Settlement

After the event ends, the market confirms the result through the UMA Optimistic Oracle:

1. A proposer submits the result and posts a bond.
2. A challenge period begins, during which other participants can dispute the result.
3. If a dispute occurs, the process can escalate to UMA voting.
4. The final winning outcome token can be redeemed for `1 USDT`.

This mechanism confirms market outcomes without requiring a single centralized adjudicator.
